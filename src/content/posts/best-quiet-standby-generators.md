Noise is the #1 neighbor complaint about standby generators — and it's almost entirely about the weekly exercise cycle, not outages. Nobody complains during a blackout. Here's how the quiet ones compare.

## The quietest picks

**[Champion 14kW aXis](/generators/champion-14kw-axis/) — 62 dB(A).** The quietest full-speed rating we've seen in home standby, thanks to serious sound dampening and a low-tone muffler. In a dense suburb, this is the one. [Check price](amazon:champion-14kw-axis).

**[Kohler 20RESCL](/generators/kohler-20rescl/) — 64 dB(A) exercise.** Kohler publishes the exercise-cycle figure separately, and 64 dB(A) is exceptional — the weekly self-test fades into background noise. [Check price](amazon:kohler-20rescl).

**[Cummins QuietConnect RS20A](/generators/cummins-rs20a/) — ≤65 dB(A).** The name is the promise: 65 dB(A) or less at 23 feet under normal load, in the most compact 20 kW box here. [Check price](amazon:cummins-rs20a).

## What the numbers mean

Manufacturers usually quote the exercise-cycle figure (the quiet one) — full-load operation is a few dB louder, and dB is logarithmic: 70 dB(A) is roughly twice as loud as 60 dB(A) to human ears. When comparing, make sure you're comparing the same measurement.

## Placement beats specs

A 67 dB(A) unit 30 feet from the property line, exhaust facing away, beats a 64 dB(A) unit tucked under the neighbor's bedroom window. Check your setback requirements and noise ordinance before finalizing placement — some suburbs cap sustained dB(A) at the lot line.
