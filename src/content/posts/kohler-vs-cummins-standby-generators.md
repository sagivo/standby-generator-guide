Generac owns the sales charts, but the premium corner of the standby generator market belongs to two industrial heavyweights: Kohler, whose Power Systems division has backed hospitals and data centers for decades, and Cummins, the diesel-engine giant whose Onan lineage has powered RVs and commercial sites for generations. If you're shopping above the budget tier, you're probably choosing between these two — most directly between the **Kohler 20RESCL** and the **Cummins RS20A**, the two 20 kW air-cooled models that anchor their residential lines.

Here's the honest breakdown: at 20 kW, the two are closer than their marketing departments would like you to believe. The real differences show up in lineup depth, noise philosophy, and load management.

## The 20 kW head-to-head: 20RESCL vs RS20A

| Spec | Kohler 20RESCL | Cummins RS20A |
|---|---|---|
| Rated kW (LP / NG) | 20 / 18 kW | 20 / 18 kW |
| Engine | Kohler OHV V-twin, 999 cc | Cummins QSJ999G V-twin, 999 cc |
| Noise | 64 dB(A) exercise rating published | 65 dB(A) at 23 ft, normal load |
| Voltage regulation | ±1.0% digital | ±1–1.5% |
| THD | <5% | <5% |
| Response time | 10 seconds | ~10 seconds (utility sensing) |
| Warranty | 5-year / 2,000-hour limited | 5-year / 2,000-hour limited |
| Fuel (NG 50%/100%) | ~161 / ~300+ cfh | 205 / 300 cfh |
| Fuel (LP 50%/100%) | ~1.9 / 3.2 gal/hr | 1.9 / 3.2 gal/hr |
| Enclosure | Aluminum, corrosion-proof | Aluminum exterior, galvanized steel interior |
| Install clearance | 18 inches from structure | 18 inches from structure |
| Wind rating | 181 mph | 180 mph (ASCE7-10) |

Look at the fuel row twice. Both brands derate ~2 kW on natural gas — the same derate trap documented in the [standby generator sizing guide](/posts/standby-generator-sizing-guide/). If you're on natural gas, both are effectively 18 kW machines, and you should do your entire load calculation against the NG rating. Fuel consumption is also nearly identical at full load (300 cubic feet per hour on NG), though Kohler edges slightly more efficient at half load on paper.

For a deeper dive on what a 20 kW unit costs to feed over a long outage, see the [standby generator fuel consumption](/posts/standby-generator-fuel-consumption/) breakdown with real spec-sheet figures for both of these models.

## Noise: Cummins' small edge

Noise is the category Cummins markets hardest, and the numbers back it up — barely. The RS20A is rated at **65 dB(A) at 23 feet under normal load**, making it one of the quietest 20 kW air-cooled units you can buy. Kohler publishes a 64 dB(A) figure for the 20RESCL's exercise mode but is less forthcoming with a single full-load number, which makes direct comparison annoying (this is exactly why the [quiet standby generators](/posts/best-quiet-standby-generators/) post warns to compare exercise-to-exercise and full-load-to-full-load).

In practice, a decibel or two at these levels is the difference between "distant conversation" and "slightly more distant conversation." Both are dramatically quieter than older air-cooled designs. If noise is your deciding factor, the RS20A has the cleaner published record.

## Load management: both, but different flavors

This is where the two philosophies diverge most clearly.

**Cummins** builds intelligent load management into the RS20A's controller, designed to cycle up to **four high-demand loads** (air conditioners, electric water heaters) so the unit carries a whole house without overloading. Cummins claims the RS20A can start and run a 5-ton A/C under full pre-load with load management engaged — a bold claim that effectively lets a 20 kW unit do work competitors assign to 24–26 kW models.

**Kohler** answers with **PowerBoost technology** plus the RDC2 controller and an RXT-series transfer switch with built-in load shedding. The 200SELS bundles manage two HVAC systems plus four non-essential loads, prioritizing the loads that matter and dropping the rest. Where Cummins cycles loads through software, Kohler's approach leans on better motor-starting kVA at the alternator level plus switch-side shedding hardware.

For a buyer, both approaches mean the same thing: you can often buy one size smaller than the "add up everything" math suggests. Run your numbers through the [kW size calculator](/tools/kw-size-calculator/) first, then ask your installer how much load management closes the gap.

## Controllers, monitoring, and the exercise cycle

- **Kohler RDC2:** a 6-button digital controller with a glare-resistant 2-line display (no more cryptic fault codes), a built-in 2.5-amp battery charger, and a real-time clock for scheduling the weekly exercise. Kohler's newer units add a 90-second EcoExercise option that runs an unloaded diagnostic test — less fuel, less noise — alongside the standard 20-minute weekly cycle. OnCue Plus remote monitoring is included via built-in Ethernet on the 26RCAL line.
- **Cummins Connect Cloud:** the RS20A includes remote monitoring out of the box — check generator status, program exercise periods, and start/stop the unit from a smartphone app, with email notifications. Its exercise mode is user-programmable and includes a patented **"crank-only" mode** that turns the engine over without a full start, reducing wear during routine self-tests.

Both controllers display service and maintenance information on-screen, which matters more than it sounds — the number-one cause of "my generator failed during the outage" is deferred maintenance, as the [standby generator maintenance](/posts/standby-generator-maintenance/) guide documents.

## The lineup-depth problem: Cummins stops at 20 kW

Here's the structural difference that decides the matchup for many buyers. **The RS20A is the top of Cummins' residential line.** There is no 22, 24, or 26 kW QuietConnect model. If your load calculation lands at 24 kW — two AC zones, electric heat, a big workshop — Cummins can't serve you in this product family.

Kohler, by contrast, runs straight up to the **26RCAL**: 26 kW on propane, 24 kW on natural gas, a 999 cc CH1006 engine, 109 amps on LP, published noise of 56 dB(A) during exercise and 67 dB(A) at full speed, and a 200-amp service-entrance-rated ATS bundled in the 200SELS package. At ~670 lbs and 47 × 26 × 32.3 inches, it's the biggest air-cooled premium unit of the two brands. If your sizing says "more than 20," the decision makes itself.

If your sizing says "less than 20," it's also worth noting both brands have smaller options — and that Generac's wider 14–26 kW air-cooled spread often wins on price, as the [Generac vs Kohler](/posts/generac-vs-kohler/) comparison covers.

## Warranty and dealer network

Both the 20RESCL and RS20A carry a **5-year / 2,000-hour limited warranty** — among the strongest in residential standby, and notably longer than Generac's base coverage. Both are backed by nationwide commercial dealer networks; Kohler leans on its Power Systems dealer channel, Cummins on its engine-service network. Practically speaking, dealer quality varies by metro more than by brand — get two or three quotes and ask each installer how many of that brand's units they service annually. The [standby generator installation cost](/posts/standby-generator-installation-cost/) post walks through what to ask at quoting time.

## Who should buy which

**Buy the Cummins RS20A if:** noise is a top-three priority, you want app-based remote monitoring included rather than as an add-on, you like the crank-only exercise philosophy, and your loads fit under 20 kW with smart load management.

**Buy the Kohler 20RESCL if:** you want the tightest published power quality (±1% voltage regulation), you prefer the load-shedding ATS hardware approach, or corrosion resistance matters (Kohler's 100% corrosion-proof enclosure is a genuine differentiator in coastal installs).

**Buy the Kohler 26RCAL if:** your load math lands at 22–26 kW. Cummins doesn't compete here, and the 26RCAL's 200-amp bundled ATS, 39 kVA motor-starting rating, and 5-year warranty make it the premium pick for larger homes.

Either way, price the *project*, not the box: unit-plus-ATS bundles for these models list around ~$5,800–$8,900 before a single wire is run, and installation (gas line, pad, electrical, permits) typically doubles the sticker. Get the install quote in writing before falling in love with a spec sheet.
