A standby generator is insurance that has to work on the worst day of the year. Maintenance is what makes sure it does — and it's about an hour a year.

## The annual checklist

1. **Oil and filter.** Every 150 hours or annually, whichever comes first. Use the weight your manual specifies (usually 5W-30 synthetic).
2. **Air filter.** Every 300 hours or annually. A clogged filter is a slow strangulation.
3. **Spark plugs.** Every 300 hours or annually. Cheap; replace, don't clean.
4. **Battery.** Test it annually, replace every 3–4 years proactively. Better: keep it on a maintainer year-round.
5. **Exercise cycle.** Confirm it's running weekly — check the controller log, don't assume.
6. **Enclosure.** Clear leaves, nests, and snow from vents. Check for rodent damage to wiring.
7. **Fuel.** For propane: check the tank gauge monthly in storm season. For NG: nothing to do.

## The failures to watch for

**Dead battery** — the #1 no-start cause, by far. A maintainer is the cheapest reliability upgrade in all of homeownership.

**Exercise-cycle faults** — if the unit throws a fault during its weekly test, don't ignore it. That's the test doing its job; call for service while the sun is shining.

**Stale firmware / controller errors** — Wi-Fi-connected units (Generac Mobile Link, Kohler OnCue) will tell you something's wrong. Check the app monthly.

## DIY vs. dealer service

The annual service is genuinely DIY-able with a [maintenance kit](/accessories/) — oil, filters, and plugs are all accessible. But an annual dealer visit ($200–$400) buys a trained eye on the transfer switch, gas connections, and controller. Many owners DIY the basics and book a dealer every 2–3 years. Either way, keep receipts: warranty claims go smoother with a paper trail.
