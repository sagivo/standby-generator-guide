The honest version of this debate: a $2,000 portable and a $12,000 standby both keep the fridge cold. The $10,000 difference buys you not having to think about it.

## The 10-year math

| | Portable (10–12 kW) | Standby (20 kW) |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment | ~$1,200–$2,500 | ~$5,500–$7,000 |
| Installation | $500–$1,500 (inlet + interlock) | $4,000–$8,000 (ATS, gas, permits) |
| Starts itself | No | Yes, in ~10 seconds |
| Fuel | Gasoline, refuel every 8–12 hrs | NG/propane, runs for days |
| Noise | 70–78 dB(A) | 62–69 dB(A) |
| Coverage | Essentials via inlet | Whole house via panel |

Run your outage hours through the [standby vs portable calculator](/tools/standby-vs-portable/) for your numbers.

## When portable wins

Outages are rare and short (under ~12 hours a year), someone is always home, and you're comfortable with extension cords or a manual transfer switch. A dual-fuel portable is genuinely capable — the trade is your labor, not your comfort.

## When standby wins

Multi-day outages. Travel. A home office that can't go dark. Medical equipment. A sump pump guarding a finished basement. An electric well. Anything where "go outside in the storm and pull the starter" isn't an acceptable plan. Standby also adds resale value; portables don't.

## The middle path

A large portable with a professionally installed manual transfer switch ($2,000–$4,000 all-in) is the value king for occasional outages. If you outgrow it, the transfer-switch wiring transfers to a future standby project.
