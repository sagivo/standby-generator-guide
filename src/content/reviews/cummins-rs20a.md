The Cummins QuietConnect RS20A is the connoisseur's standby generator: commercial Cummins/Onan engineering, the most compact 20 kW footprint we've seen, and quiet operation — at a premium price through a dealer channel. If reliability is your religion, start here.

## Who the RS20A is for

Buy it if you want the most overbuilt 20 kW unit and you're comfortable buying through a dealer rather than one-click retail. It's also the pick for tight lots: it installs as close as 18 inches from the home.

## Engineering notes

Cummins builds engines for trucks, trains, and data centers. The RS20A inherits that DNA: full-pressure lubrication, intelligent load management (kits sold separately) for heavy motor loads, and an aluminum-over-galvanized-steel enclosure. It starts a 5-ton AC with a full household load — the hardest test in this class.

## The dealer caveat

The transfer switch is typically sold and quoted separately, and street pricing is less transparent than Generac/Kohler. Get an installed quote from a Cummins dealer and compare the all-in number — the generator premium narrows when installation is bundled.

## Ownership notes

- **Warranty:** 5-year limited.
- **Monitoring:** Cloud Connect smartphone app for remote status and control.
- **Cold weather:** Runs at 0°F out of the box; cold-weather kits extend it further.
- **Footprint:** 34.1" × 36" × 27.3" — remarkably compact for 20 kW.

## Fuel consumption

Published figures: ~172 cu-ft/hr of natural gas at half load, ~268 at full; ~1.8 gal/hr of propane at half, ~3.1 at full. That's among the thriftier 20 kW units on propane — relevant if you expect long outage runs. See the [fuel consumption guide](/posts/standby-generator-fuel-consumption/) for per-hour cost math.

## How it stacks up

Against the [Kohler 20RESCL](/generators/kohler-20rescl/): similar premium positioning, but Cummins brings the industrial pedigree while Kohler brings the quieter exercise cycle (64 dB(A)) and bigger residential network. Against the [Generac 22kW](/generators/generac-guardian-22kw/): the Cummins costs more per kW but the engineering margins are visibly heavier — thicker enclosure, bigger alternator headroom. For buyers who keep equipment for decades and maintain it properly, that's a reasonable trade. For buyers who want the cheapest competent 20 kW, it isn't.

## Load management and installation

The RS20A's intelligent load management handles up to four circuits, with expansion kits for more — plan your priority loads (AC, well pump, water heater) with the installer upfront. At 34.1" × 36" it's unusually compact for 20 kW, and the 18-inch-to-structure clearance (NFPA 37 compliant) makes tight lots workable where bigger boxes won't fit. The ATS is typically quoted separately, so compare installed quotes apples-to-apples: generator + ATS + gas + electrical + pad + permits. Cold-climate buyers: it runs at 0°F out of the box, and the available cold-weather kit extends that further.

## The bottom line on value

The RS20A costs more than a Generac 22kW-class unit while offering less nameplate power — you're paying for engineering margins, not kilowatts. For buyers who maintain equipment meticulously and keep it for 20+ years, that trade pays off in fewer mid-life repairs. For buyers who want maximum backup per dollar today, the [Generac Guardian 22kW](/generators/generac-guardian-22kw/) or [24kW](/generators/generac-guardian-24kw/) delivers more house for less money. Know which buyer you are before you fall for the spec sheet.
