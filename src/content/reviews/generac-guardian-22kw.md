The Generac Guardian 22kW is the best-selling whole-house generator size in America, and it's easy to see why: it's the smallest unit that credibly covers a 2,500 sq ft home with central air, at a mid-range price with the full Guardian feature set.

## Who the Guardian 22kW is for

Buy it if your home is around 2,500 sq ft with central air and your load calculation lands at or under 22 kW with margin. This is the default recommendation from most installers for a reason — it hits the fat part of the bell curve.

Skip it (up) if you have two large AC units plus electric heat or a big workshop; skip it (down) to the [18kW](/generators/generac-guardian-18kw/) if your loads are modest and you'd rather save the money.

## Power and fuel

The G-Force 1000 Series V-twin delivers 22 kW on either natural gas or propane. Automatic start, automatic transfer, runs until utility power returns — the whole point of standby.

## What you get in the box

200A transfer switch: included. Wi-Fi monitoring: included. 5-year warranty: included. The Guardian line's value proposition is that there's very little left to buy — compare that against competitors that sell the ATS separately.

## Ownership notes

- **Installer familiarity:** The 22kW is the unit most electricians have installed dozens of — quotes are competitive and surprises are rare.
- **Maintenance:** Annual oil/filter service; exercise cycle is automatic and quiet-ish at ~67 dB(A).
- **Battery:** Not included. Add a crank battery and a maintainer.

## The natural-gas caveat

Rated 22 kW on propane, **19.5 kW on natural gas**. Most suburban buyers are on NG, so do your math against 19.5 — a 20 kW load calculation means you need the [24kW](/generators/generac-guardian-24kw/), not this one. This single spec causes more undersizing mistakes than any other in home standby. When in doubt, run the [kW size calculator](/tools/kw-size-calculator/) with your fuel selected.

## Long-term ownership

The 22kW is Generac's highest-volume standby unit, which is an ownership advantage in itself: every electrician has installed dozens, parts are everywhere, and there's a deep bench of owner experience online for troubleshooting. Expect 15–25 years of service with annual maintenance — oil and filter yearly, plugs and air filter per the manual, battery on a maintainer. The 5-year warranty has no hour cap, which matters in long-outage regions. For the full reliability picture, see [Generac vs Kohler reliability](/posts/generac-vs-kohler-reliability/).

## Installation planning

Standard Guardian install: 200A service-entrance ATS tied to your meter/main, composite pad, dedicated gas line, permits and inspection. Most projects land $10,000–$15,000 all-in — the [installation cost estimator](/tools/install-cost-estimator/) breaks down your scenario. Two things to confirm before install day: gas meter capacity with the utility (a 22 kW at full load wants ~230+ cu-ft/hr plus the house), and truck access to the pad site for the freight delivery. Neither is difficult; both are miserable as surprises.

## Why it's the default recommendation

Installers recommend the 22kW more than any other size because it hits the fat part of the bell curve: enough for the median American home with margin, cheap enough to quote without apology, familiar enough that every tech has installed dozens. It's not the most exciting pick in the lineup — the [24kW](/generators/generac-guardian-24kw/) is more efficient, the [26kW](/generators/generac-guardian-26kw/) more capable — but it's the one your installer can quote, deliver, and service fastest.
